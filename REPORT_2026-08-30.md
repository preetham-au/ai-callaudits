are not scored. Everything below refers to those 168 unless stated.

Method mirrors the reviewers' 27-Aug sheet: for each call, did the agent say
the injected pre-call values correctly, did it follow the prompt's workflow,
and does the disposition match what actually happened.

Counts cover only calls that reached the step where a value is spoken. A

### The expiry date is invented, not misread

| injected `red` | what the agent said | interaction |
|---|---|---|
| `30-Aug` | "चार सितंबर" (4 September) | 5740886 |
| `30-Aug` | "चार September" | 5740899 |
| `02-Sep` | "चार सितंबर" (4 September) | 5741206 |
| `01-Sep` | "four September" | 5741222 |
| `01-Sep` | "eleven August" | 5741471 |

Three customers with three different real dates were each told **4 September**.
The only date-bearing values injected into those calls are `red` and
`base_month`; "4 September" appears in neither, so the model is generating a
date rather than picking up an adjacent field. Checked for a bad time window:
there isn't one — 70 of 71 date checks fall in the same hour.

Same fault the reviewers logged on 27-Aug as *"Incorect RED shared"* and
*"DTD and RED incorrect"*, so it is at minimum a four-day-old recurring defect,
and the highest-severity finding here: a customer acting on it renews against
the wrong deadline.

### Coverage limit — what was NOT checked

The audit verifies **8 variables, not the ~90 injected**. The rest — add-on
premiums, `basic_od`, `basic_tp`, `new_total_value_idv`, `branch`, `imd_code`,
the entire add-on block — are ones the prompt requires the agent to speak
*only if the customer asks*. Just 13 calls reached the payment step and 7
reached objections, so those paths were barely exercised and there is
**no evidence either way** on whether add-on or premium-breakup values are read
correctly. Establishing that needs a day with more deep conversations, or
seeded test calls that deliberately ask for a breakup.

## 2. Where calls actually die

| step | reached | of 168 |

**72% of connected calls end during the greeting.** Nothing downstream —
premium accuracy, link delivery, objection handling — can move the day's
outcome much while the opener loses three of four customers. Largest number in
this report, and it is not an audit failure; it is where the business is losing
the conversation.

## 4. Tamil (agent 127)

16 of the 168 audited calls, in the same sheet, transcripts in Tamil script.

| | Hindi 125 | Tamil 127 |
|---|---|---|
| audited | 152 | 16 |
| reached step 2 | 42 | 5 |
| live variable checks | 375 | 47 |
| wrong values | 5 (all `red`) | **0** |
| omissions | 8 | 2 (both interruptions) |
| disposition findings | 20 | 2 |

The zero was verified rather than assumed — a matcher that could not read Tamil
would produce the same zero. 47 checks ran and matched with cited evidence. The
reason Tamil reads clean is that the agent speaks the *values* in English inside
Tamil sentences, so they match in Latin script:

> `...Chola MS policy one September இந்த வருஷம் க்கு expire ஆகுது...` (`01-Sep`)
> `...renewal premium eight thousand four hundred seventy two rupees...`

**The date hallucination did not occur on any Tamil call** — all 5 Tamil `red`
checks are correct.

But only 5 of 16 Tamil calls got past the greeting, so 47 checks is too thin to
call Tamil clean. Treat "no Tamil verification errors" as *not yet contradicted*
rather than *verified*. The 2 Tamil findings are both disposition, which is
language-independent and therefore as trustworthy as the Hindi ones.

---

## 5. What to fix, in order

1. **The expiry date.** One variable, hallucinated, customer-facing, already
   flagged by humans four days earlier. Pin `red` to the injected value — the
   prompt already forbids the agent from converting it.
2. **`voicemail_ivr` classification.** 13 misfiled live conversations in one
   day. Wrong routing, silent cost. Platform-side, not agent-side.
3. **The step-1 opener.** 72% drop-off dwarfs everything else here.

---

## Confidence — read before acting

**§3 disposition is fitted, not earned.** The checks agree 20/20 with the
reviewers' labelled calls, but all 13 of their findings were one of four
transcript contradictions, and those four rules were then written from that same
set of 20. They reliably catch the failure modes your team has already
characterised; they are unproven against a fifth. Strong signal, not a
measurement.

**§1 variables agree 15/20** with the reviewers, and 3 of the 5 disagreements
look like engine wins: all three are `red = "Friday, August 21, 2026"` calls
where the agent said "चार August" — the reviewers caught that on a fourth call
with the same value and missed these three. The two genuine misses needed
knowledge from outside the data feed.

**Two known engine distortions**, both inflating the warn count and both from
the same root cause — the checker does not ask *why* a value is absent:
118 variables were scored as missed on calls that never reached the step, and
9 of 10 omissions are interruptions. The per-call CSV is unaffected (no row is
flagged `Verfication Error` for an omission), but the score column is.

**The Tamil rubric was built without a Tamil reader.** Structure and fatal codes
come from the prompt's own English rule text; paraphrases of Tamil lines are
unverified. A Tamil speaker reading the 5 step-2 transcripts against the
injected values would settle §4 in ten minutes.
